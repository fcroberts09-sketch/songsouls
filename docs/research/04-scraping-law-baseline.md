# Baseline price fetcher: legal, practical and policy findings (research date 2026-09-22)

Scope: low-volume, on-demand, headless-browser fetches of public product pages on walmart.com, heb.com, instacart.com, kroger.com, amazon.com (plus Uber/Lyft fares), from a fresh anonymous session, identified UA, robots.txt-respecting, no login, no CAPTCHA solving.

## 1. US law

**F1. Scraping public pages without login is outside the CFAA in the Ninth Circuit.** hiQ v. LinkedIn (9th Cir. Apr 18, 2022, No. 17-16783, on remand after Van Buren): public websites have "no gates to lift or lower," so "without authorization" does not apply; the court pointed plaintiffs to contract/tort theories instead. https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/04/18/17-16783.pdf ; https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2022/04/scraping-public-websites-still-isnt-crime-court-appeals-declares — Certain.

**F2. But hiQ still lost on contract/consent facts.** Dec 2022 consent judgment: $500k, permanent injunction, data/code deletion, and hiQ stipulated LinkedIn could establish CFAA and Cal. Penal Code §502 liability — because hiQ had used fake accounts/"turkers" (logged-in access) and continued after a cease-and-desist. https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/ ; https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-learned/ — Certain.

**F3. Van Buren v. United States (June 3, 2021, 6-3)**: CFAA "exceeds authorized access" is a "gates-up-or-down" inquiry; violating purpose-based rules (ToS) with otherwise-permitted access is not a CFAA violation. https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf ; https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB10616 — Certain.

**F4. Meta v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal. Jan 23, 2024, Judge Chen, No. 3:23-cv-00077-EMC)**: summary judgment for the scraper; Meta's terms "do not bar logged-off scraping of public data," so a ToS only reaches you while you are a logged-in user bound by it. This is the closest analogue to our case (public page, no account). https://www.fbm.com/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/ ; https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2024/01/24/california-court-issues-noteworthy-decision-on-breach-of-contract-claims-in-web-scraping-dispute/ — Certain.

**F5. X Corp. v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal. No. 3:23-cv-03698, Judge Alsup)**: May 9, 2024 dismissal of contract, trespass, misappropriation, unjust-enrichment claims (Copyright Act preemption; warning about "information monopolies"). Dec 2024: X allowed to replead trespass to chattels on server-impairment allegations; trial was calendared for Mar 2, 2026 (outcome not found). Lesson: measurable server burden is the surviving theory, so volume matters. https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-tosses-xs-contract-claims-against-data-scraping-company/ ; https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/401510/x-corp-can-revive-scraping-battle-with-bright-dat.html — Certain/Likely on trial status.

**F6. Ryanair v. Booking.com (D. Del.)**: July 2024 jury found CFAA violation "with intent to defraud," $5,000 damages (the statutory minimum). Facts were logged-in scraping via myRyanair accounts by Booking's partners, continuing after cease-and-desist. Jan 31, 2025 the judge set the verdict aside for failure to prove $5,000 loss; appeal pending in the Third Circuit (EFF amicus July 2025). Takeaway: authenticated scraping plus post-notice continuation is the CFAA danger zone; public-page fetching is not. https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/07/ryanairs-cfaa-claim-against-bookingcom-has-nothing-do-actual-hacking ; https://www.irishlegal.com/articles/judge-overturns-ryanairs-us-court-win-against-bookingcom ; https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/08/ryanair-v-booking-cfaa-trial-ends-with-strangest-possible-outcome-guest-blog-post.htm — Certain.

**F7. 2026: Amazon v. Perplexity (N.D. Cal. No. 3:25-cv-09514; 9th Cir. No. 26-1444, Aug 4, 2026)**. PI granted Mar 9, 2026 against Perplexity's Comet agent (logged into users' Amazon accounts, no identifying UA). Ninth Circuit vacated: when a user directs an agent, "it is the user who accessed" Amazon; "an AI agent is not a person" under the CFAA; CFAA/CDAFA unlikely to succeed; contract, DMCA and tort theories expressly left open. Note our fetcher is server-side, so this "user accessed" reasoning does not protect us; our protection is F1/F4 (public, no login). https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-06-ninth-circuit-rules-on-ai-agent-access-to-third-party-websites-under-cfaa ; https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/04/26-1444.pdf — Certain.

**F8. 2025-26: Google v. SerpApi (N.D. Cal., filed Dec 19, 2025)**: DMCA §1201 theory built on circumventing "SearchGuard" (a CAPTCHA-like TPM). Core claim dismissed with prejudice July 20, 2026 (no copyrighted content); narrowed refiling Aug 10, 2026. Lesson: CAPTCHA/TPM circumvention is what invites §1201 claims; a no-CAPTCHA-solving rule avoids this entirely. https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/12/26/google-sues-serpapi-parasitic-scraping-circumvention-protection-measures/ ; https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2026/02/21/serpapi-asks-court-to-dismiss-google-web-scraping-lawsuit/4591023 — Likely (post-July status from secondary reporting).

**F9. Texas angle.** Texas Harmful Access by Computer Act (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code ch. 143, keyed to Penal Code §33.02 "access without effective consent") has been argued to reach ToU-violating scraping of a public site. Southwest v. Kiwi.com (N.D. Tex. Sept 30, 2021) granted a preliminary injunction on breach of contract alone; Kiwi was bound because it bought tickets on the site and received cease-and-desist letters. H-E-B's terms select Texas law and Bexar County courts. https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=articles&Template=%2FCM%2FHTMLDisplay.cfm&ContentID=36366 ; https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2021/10/04/southwest-airlines-wins-injunction-barring-travel-site-from-scraping/ — Likely (HACA-on-public-site theory is bar-journal commentary, not settled appellate law).

**F10. Public vs authenticated — the line.** Public, no-login, no-circumvention fetching: CFAA/CDAFA very unlikely (F1,F3,F4,F7); contract claims require assent, which is weak for browsewrap but becomes strong after a cease-and-desist or any account/purchase (F2,F9); trespass to chattels needs provable server burden (F5). Authenticated or CAPTCHA-solving fetching flips all three (F2,F6,F8). — Certain.

## 2. Retailer terms, robots.txt, official APIs

**F11. Walmart ToU** prohibits "any robot, spider, site search/retrieval application or other manual or automatic device to retrieve, index, 'scrape,' 'data mine' or otherwise gather any Materials" without "express prior written consent," plus an AI-training clause. The page itself served us a PerimeterX "press and hold" CAPTCHA. https://www.walmart.com/help/article/walmart-com-terms-of-use/3b75080af40340d6bbd596f116fae5a0 — Likely (quote via secondary sources; page blocked to fetcher).
robots.txt (fetched): `User-agent: *` disallows `/search`, `/api/`, `/typeahead/`, `/store/ajax/*`, `/store/electrode/api/*`, `*/api/wpa`, `/orders`; product pages `/ip/…` are allowed; `Crawl-delay: 5` only for Slurp. https://www.walmart.com/robots.txt — Certain.
API: walmart.io Affiliate/"Content Provider" API exposes product lookup with "localized price and availability based on zip code," read-only, approval "selective" and tied to the affiliate program (Impact). Marketplace/Supplier APIs are partner-only. https://walmart.io/docs/affiliates/v1/product-lookup ; https://vorplabs.com/agent-tools/walmart-marketplace-api — Likely.

**F12. H-E-B Terms (effective Aug 8, 2024)** verbatim: prohibit use of "any robot, spider, site search/retrieval application or other manual or automatic device to retrieve, index, 'scrape,' 'data mine' or otherwise gather Service content… without H-E-B's express prior written consent," with a carve-out only for public search engines building indices "but not caches or archives." Texas law; Bexar County exclusive venue; no arbitration clause reported. https://www.heb.com/terms — Certain.
robots.txt (fetched): disallows `/search`, `/graphql`, `*/ajax/*`, `/category/*?*`, `/brand/*`, `/cart/`, `/my-account/`; `/product-detail/<slug>/<id>` is allowed (a product page returned 200 to a plain curl). No official product/price API found. https://www.heb.com/robots.txt — Certain.

**F13. Instacart Terms (May 7, 2026)** §4.3: do not "scrape, crawl, data-mine, collect, harvest, or otherwise access the Services or their content except through interfaces expressly authorized"; separate clause bars automated access "in a manner that circumvents or disregards Instacart's robots.txt"; robots.txt allowances for search "do not… authorize any Automated System… for agentic browsing… or any other commercial purpose"; automated systems must "clearly identify [themselves]." FAA arbitration; Delaware law. https://www.instacart.com/terms — Certain.
robots.txt (fetched, "Last Updated 02-20-2026"): header says "Any bot scraping or crawling this site must abide by Terms of Service"; named allow-groups only for Googlebot, Bingbot, Slurp, DuckDuckGo, Applebot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot/Perplexity-User, social unfurlers; even they are disallowed from `/store/item*`, `/api/`, `/rest/v8/`; final group is `User-Agent: *` / `Disallow: /`. A robots-respecting fetcher therefore may not fetch instacart.com at all. https://www.instacart.com/robots.txt — Certain.
API: Instacart Developer Platform requires an application (30-40 days to production keys); Developer Terms (July 3, 2024) forbid scraping, forbid apps that "display priced items from multiple retailers on the same screen," and apps whose purpose is "to migrate end users off of Instacart." Unusable for a comparison baseline. https://docs.instacart.com/developer_platform_api/guide/terms_and_policies/developer_terms/ — Certain.

**F14. Kroger.** kroger.com terms and robots.txt could not be retrieved (Akamai returned 403/503 to every fetch; robots.txt timed out). Corporate terms say no part of the site "may be reproduced… without the express written permission of Kroger." https://www.thekrogerco.com/terms-conditions/ — Likely.
API: Kroger Developer Public Products API — OAuth2 client_credentials, scope `product.compact`; `filter.locationId` required to get `price.regular`/`price.promo`/`stockLevel`; "all public APIs have a daily rate limit applied equally across all clients." This is the only free, self-serve, no-affiliate, store-localized price API in the set. https://developer.kroger.com/reference/api/product-api-public ; https://developer.kroger.com/support/faq ; https://www.postman.com/kroger/the-kroger-co-s-public-workspace/documentation/ki6utqb/kroger-public-apis — Certain (existence/shape), Guessing on quota number.

**F15. Amazon Conditions of Use** (mirror text, updated Aug 6, 2025): "you may not utilize any data mining, robots, or similar data gathering and extraction tools to extract (whether once or many times) for re-utilization any parts of the content of any Amazon Service"; "You may not… create and/or publish your own database that features substantial parts of any Amazon Service (e.g. our prices)." Arbitration, King County WA. https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GLSBYFE9MGKKQXXM ; https://ship.amazon.com/site-terms — Certain.
robots.txt (fetched): `/dp/` product pages allowed; disallows `/gp/offer-listing/`, `/dp/product-availability/`, `/gp/cart`, sign-in, review-writing; explicit groups for dozens of AI crawlers (AI2Bot, PerplexityBot, Brightbot…). https://www.amazon.com/robots.txt — Certain.
API: PA-API 5 retired May 15, 2026; replacement Creators API requires an Associates account with 10 qualifying sales per trailing 30 days and OAuth; data must drive traffic to Amazon. https://webservices.amazon.com/paapi5/documentation/register-for-pa-api.html ; https://www.keywordrush.com/blog/amazon-creator-api-what-changed-and-how-to-switch/ — Likely.

**F16. Uber/Lyft.** Uber `GET /estimates/price`: "Access to this API endpoint requires approval from Uber," and "using this endpoint for price comparisons with competing services violates the API Terms of Use." Deep links `https://m.uber.com/looking?client_id=…&pickup={json}&drop[0]={json}&product_id=…` open the app; the URL carries no fare. Lyft's developer portal now redirects to a login wall. https://developer.uber.com/docs/riders/references/api/v1.2/estimates-price-get ; https://developer.uber.com/docs/riders/ride-requests/tutorials/deep-links/introduction — Certain (Uber), Guessing (Lyft program closed).

## 3. Practical

**F17. Bot management observed 2026-09-22 (my curl probes, datacenter egress, browser UA):** Walmart: Akamai edge + PerimeterX/HUMAN (px-cloud, reCAPTCHA markers; "press and hold" challenge). Kroger: `Server: AkamaiGHost` 403 on the homepage; robots.txt unreachable. H-E-B: Imperva Incapsula markers, but homepage and a product page returned 200. Instacart: CloudFront + nginx, reCAPTCHA markers, JS-rendered session pricing. Amazon: CloudFront, 202/405 non-page responses (bot block). Corroboration: https://scrapeops.io/websites/walmart/ ; https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/how-to-build-a-grocery-price-comparison-tool-with-python — Certain for observed headers, Likely for vendor attribution.

**F18. Location-dependent pricing without an account.** Walmart: store/ZIP lives in cookies `locGuestData`/`locDataV3` (base64 JSON with `nodeId`, `postalCode.base`, `storeIntent`), `assortmentStoreId`; store IDs are discoverable from the public store directory; without them you get "default national" prices. https://serpapi.com/blog/scrape-walmart-search-results-for-a-specific-store-id-with-plain-http-requests/ — Certain. H-E-B, Kroger and Instacart all expose guest store/ZIP pickers that set a session cookie before login; Amazon uses the "deliver to" ZIP (glow) cookie. — Likely (not individually verified this session).

**F19. How price-intelligence vendors get data.** Datasembly: GPI covers "over 150,000 stores and over 200 retail banners… 30,000 zip codes," ~1B observations/day, weekly, top-1,000 products by store coverage, base = first week Oct 2019; describes its pricing as "same-as-in-store… collected from public sources, such as websites and mobile apps." https://datasembly.com/grocery-price-index/ ; https://datasembly.com/grocery-price-index-2-0-has-arrived/ — Certain on coverage, Likely on the "public sources" quote. Price2Spy openly advertises CAPTCHA circumvention and "Stealth IP." https://www.price2spy.com/feature-comparison.html — Certain. Camelcamelcamel "agreed to Amazon's request to stop tracking prices in select regions" during COVID — a compliance-on-request pattern, no lawsuit. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camelcamelcamel — Certain. Bright Data is the defendant that has won (F4,F5). No lawsuit found against Datasembly, Keepa, Wiser, Prisync, Competera, Price2Spy. Capital One Shopping/Honey litigation (settled Sept 2025) concerns affiliate-cookie replacement, not scraping. https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2025/09/19/333051.htm — Certain.

## 4. Norms

**F20. RFC 9309**: product token = letters/`_`/`-`; SHOULD appear in the User-Agent; UA "SHOULD describe the purpose of the crawler"; matching case-insensitive; `Disallow: /` under `*` means stay out. https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9309 — Certain. Common Crawl: identifies as `CCBot/2.0 (https://commoncrawl.org/faq/)`, obeys robots.txt, publishes IP ranges, runs an opt-out registry. https://commoncrawl.org/ccbot — Certain. Internet Archive (Apr 2017) deprioritizes robots.txt for archiving but honors emailed opt-outs — the "archival exception," not a model for commercial fetchers. https://blog.archive.org/2017/04/17/robots-txt-meant-for-search-engines-dont-work-well-for-web-archives/ — Certain. IMC 2025 study of 130 self-declared bots: compliance drops as directives get stricter. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3730567.3764471 — Certain. Research ethics framework (Brown et al., Big Data & Society 2025) treats prior cease-and-desist letters and restrictive robots.txt as red flags. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20539517251381686 — Certain. Cease-and-desist playbook that the case law rewards: stop the named activity immediately, never create accounts to route around it (hiQ, Kiwi, Booking all lost on exactly that), delete on request (camel), keep a contact address in the UA.

## 5. Mobile capture

**F21.** iOS: an app extension and its containing app "have no direct access to each other's containers"; a Share extension receives only `NSExtensionItem` attachments (web URL, text, image) declared via `NSExtensionActivationSupportsWebURLWithMaxCount`/`…ImageWithMaxCount`. So it cannot read Instacart/H-E-B/Walmart cookies or logged-in prices, but it can receive a screenshot the user shares and OCR it on-device. https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/General/Conceptual/ExtensibilityPG/ExtensionScenarios.html — Certain. Android: `ACTION_SEND` delivers only `EXTRA_TEXT` / `EXTRA_STREAM` content URIs; no sender data. https://developer.android.com/training/sharing/receive — Certain.
Shared URLs: Walmart `walmart.com/ip/<slug>/<itemId>` (numeric item ID); H-E-B `heb.com/product-detail/<slug>/<productId>` (verified 200 for ID 315286); Instacart product links carry a product/item ID but are typically Branch `instacart.app.link` redirects and land on paths robots.txt disallows even to Google. — Likely/Guessing (Instacart). Uber/Lyft: no shareable fare URL; Uber deep links carry pickup/drop only; Lyft "share ride" links are live-location safety links. — Certain (Uber), Likely (Lyft).

## Policy the product should adopt (docs/CLEANROOM_POLICY.md)

- Public pages only: never log in, never create or borrow accounts, never reuse a user's cookies or tokens server-side.
- Never solve, relay, or bypass a CAPTCHA, "press and hold," or JS challenge; a challenge response is a hard stop for that domain for 24h and is logged.
- Parse and obey robots.txt per RFC 9309 before every crawl session; a `Disallow: /` for `*` (Instacart today) disables the domain automatically; disallowed paths (`/search`, `/api/`, `/graphql`, `/typeahead`, `/store/ajax`) are never requested — product-detail URLs only.
- Identified User-Agent: `<ProductName>PriceCheck/<ver> (+https://<us>/bot; mailto:bot@<us>)`; product token in robots lookups; no UA spoofing, no residential/rotating proxies, static published egress IPs with reverse DNS.
- Rate limits: max 1 request per domain per 10 s, max 200 product fetches per domain per day, only on user demand, no scheduled full-catalog crawls; Retry-After and 429/403 honored with exponential backoff and circuit breaker.
- Store only the fields needed (price, unit, timestamp, store/ZIP, product ID); do not republish a bulk price database; retain raw HTML at most 24h.
- Prefer official APIs when they exist (Kroger Public API first; Walmart affiliate API on approval); never use an API in breach of its terms (Instacart, Uber comparison bans).
- Location: set store/ZIP via the site's guest picker cookies only; never a user's authenticated location.
- Cease-and-desist protocol: any written objection from a domain owner disables that domain the same day, data from it is purged on request, and counsel is notified; no re-entry via new IPs or accounts.
- Mobile: share extensions read only what the user shares (URL or screenshot); OCR runs on-device; no attempt to read other apps' data.
- Legal review gate: H-E-B (Texas HACA/venue) and Walmart require sign-off before enablement; Amazon, Instacart, Uber/Lyft are off by default.
