# Competitive landscape & market validation: same-retailer, cross-customer price-comparison app (as of 22 Sept 2026)

Method note: ~40 web searches plus ~30 page fetches. Search budget was exhausted before I could run a few planned queries (listed at the end). Two primary PDFs (House E&C Aug-11-2026 letter; Hedges Senate testimony) could not be text-extracted in this sandbox, so they are cited only via secondary coverage.

## 1. Does anything like this exist? (Direct competitors)

**F1. No shipped, funded product does same-retailer cross-customer price comparison at scale in the US.** Every consumer price tool I found is either (a) cross-retailer (is it cheaper elsewhere?), (b) price-history for one listing (Keepa/camelcamelcamel), or (c) coupon/cashback. None surfaces "the lowest price *other shoppers* were shown at *this* retailer for *this* item." Evidence: general 2026 roundups of price extensions list only cross-retailer/history tools (https://www.cheaperly.ai/blog/best-price-tracker-browser-extensions-2026, https://harpa.ai/blog/best-price-tracker-browser-extensions). Confidence: **Likely** (absence of evidence after broad search; not provable).

**F2. Closest thing: "Natural Price" (github.com/Tumub/natural-price)** — open-source (AGPL-3.0) Chrome/Firefox MV3 extension that "shows the price a stranger would get next to the price you were shown." Architecture is almost exactly the proposed product's two baselines: (1) a "clean-room" fetch of the same page with no cookies/login (local private tab, or an optional Playwright "fetch-service"), and (2) an optional "crowd-api" aggregating anonymous observations to show "what other people saw for the same product in the same hour." Status: "private beta open, no hosted service," 17 commits, 0 stars, not in any store, launch sites are European (IKEA, MediaMarkt CH, Nike, Booking.com). No company, no funding. URL: https://github.com/Tumub/natural-price. Confidence: **Certain** on facts; it is a hobby project, not a competitor — but it shows the idea is obvious enough to be independently built.

**F3. Northeastern's $heriff (Mislove/Wilson) — dead.** Launched Dec 2016; compared your price against the lab's own proxy personas (not other users) on Amazon, Google Flights, Priceline; found little price discrimination, more "price steering." Manual site returns 503 today. https://news.northeastern.edu/2016/12/12/new-web-tool-helps-online-shoppers-detect-price-discrimination-on-e-commerce-sites/ ; http://sheriff-v2.dynu.net/views/manual (503). Confidence: **Certain** (dead), **Likely** (persona-based not cross-user).

**F4. Consumer Reports has crowdsourced the exact data manually, twice, but has no tool.** Instacart study (Sept 2025 fieldwork, published Dec 9 2025): 437 recruited, 193 usable submissions, volunteers on video calls adding 18–20 identical items simultaneously and screenshotting; 74% of items had multiple prices, avg 13% spread, max 23%, basket spread ~7%, "about $1,200/yr" for a family of four; one Target store had seven price tiers ($81.24–$86.78). https://groundworkcollaborative.org/work/instacart/ . Uber/Lyft study (June 2026): 174 volunteers, 18 states, 30 virtual routes priced within ~6 minutes, median lowest-to-highest spread 42.4% (headline reported as ~50% before Uber-prompted corrections), 12.4% of discounts were false-reference pricing. https://www.consumerreports.org/money/questionable-business-practices/uber-lyft-different-prices-for-same-ride-and-fake-discounts-a1093538909/ . CR's only shipped tool, the "Price Tracker" (Feb 2026), is cross-retailer history for 16 products. https://www.consumerreports.org/money/shop-smarter-with-the-consumer-reports-price-tracker-2026-a1112175166/ . Confidence: **Certain**.

**F5. Local TV (WPXI, 2025) replicated the method by hand** — dozens of shoppers in 8 cities checking 6 products 3x/day, some logged in, some anonymous; found a Samsung TV at Walmart at $877.95/$898 vs $1,047.90 for one Atlanta shopper, and a Lowe's Blackstone grill at $299 vs $369 in Orlando. https://www.wpxi.com/news/investigates/what-is-price-profiling-how-could-it-cost-you-more-when-shopping-online/F5M3EXX3LVF7VCWJWNLEYQKUGQ/ . Confidence: **Certain**. Journalists clearly want this data and are producing it manually.

**F6. Mozilla/EFF/The Markup/AlgorithmWatch/Which?: advocacy and explainers only, no tool.** Mozilla Foundation "Nothing Personal" explainer (https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/nothing-personal/surveillance-dynamic-pricing-legal-groceries-tickets/); EFF July 2026 blog backing CA A.B. 2654 (https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/07/san-francisco-dont-fall-industry-defense-surveillance-pricing); AlgorithmWatch's DataSkop data-donation platform targets recommender systems, not pricing (https://algorithmwatch.org/en/dataskop/). No Which?/Consumentenbond price-donation tool found. Confidence: **Likely**.

**F7. "Fairprice Guru" (App Store id6762501012)** is an AI bill analyzer (medical, contractor, vet invoices vs. "market rate") — not retail, not cross-customer. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fairprice-guru-price-cheker/id6762501012 . Confidence: **Certain**. No YC/Product Hunt launches of a personalized-pricing detector 2024–26 surfaced.

**F8. Basket (basketsavings.com)** is the only live crowdsourced grocery-price app; it crowdsources *shelf* prices to compare *stores*, not per-user online prices at one store. Alive in 2026 comparisons. https://basketsavings.com/index.html . Confidence: **Likely**.

## 3. B2B data / price-intelligence players

**F12. No incumbent sells a "personalized pricing compliance" or NY-disclosure audit product yet.** Datasembly (retail price/promo data; MAP compliance; Numerator TPR partnership) — https://consumergoods.com/datasembly-numerator-partner-help-cgs-pricing-promotions-and-sales-lift ; Competera/Prisync sell repricing *to* retailers (Prisync ~$99/mo; Competera targets $300M+ retailers) — https://www.symson.com/comparison-dynamic-pricing-software-prisync-vs-competera-vs-symson . Law-firm compliance guides (Holland & Knight Apr 2026; Faegre May 2026) recommend "pricing data mapping," "fee and price transparency audits," "A/B governance" but name **no vendors or tools** — https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/surveillance-pricing-ai-pricing-tools ; https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/5/surveillance-pricing-the-next-frontier-of-privacy-litigation . **F18. Personalization is migrating to promotions/loyalty, which every enacted law exempts** (CT, NY One Fair Price, Gottheimer's No Rigged Grocery Prices Act all carve out "discounts, promotions, loyalty programs") — https://gottheimer.house.gov/posts/release-gottheimer-announces-new-bipartisan-legislation-to-ban-ai-surveillance-pricing-at-grocery-stores-delivery-services ; Uber lobbies that NY's ban "would kill promotions"; NY AG notes Instacart partners "may continue to test promotions and discounts." Implication: a detector must compare *net* price after personalized offers, and the legal hook there is weaker. Confidence: **Likely**.

## Verdict on whitespace
**Yes — same-retailer, cross-customer comparison is unoccupied as of Sept 2026 by any funded or shipped product.** The three closest things:
1. **Natural Price (Tumub, GitHub)** — same architecture (clean-room baseline + crowd median), but unfunded private beta, EU sites, 0 stars. Same *thing*, no *company*.
2. **Consumer Reports / Groundwork / More Perfect Union** — same *data* (simultaneous multi-user baskets) collected manually 2x/yr with 174–437 volunteers; a nonprofit, not a product, and no continuous collection. They are the most likely to fund or partner on a tool rather than build one.
3. **Keepa / camelcamelcamel / CR Price Tracker / Basket** — price *history* or *cross-store* comparison; do not observe per-user prices. Datasembly could add an anonymous-baseline layer for B2B but lacks logged-in consumer observations.
Caveat: whitespace partly exists because the obvious retail target (Instacart item tests) has vanished and remaining variation (rideshare, airlines, promos) is harder to attribute to personal data.

## Demand evidence ranked
1. **Evidence licensing to plaintiffs/regulators — strongest.** Two JetBlue class actions and the WaPo case were filed on thin evidence (a tweet, a renewal email); plaintiffs' counsel say on record they cannot tell who was harmed; NJ's PRA (eff. Aug 2027) and 24 pending state bills with statutory damages make cross-customer proof directly monetizable; FTC Section 5 policy (Aug 2026), CA sweep, NY AG letters, and 30+ congressional letters all ask the same question ("are two consumers seeing different prices?"). Firms are soliciting clients but have no data.
2. **NY-disclosure compliance audits — moderate, latent.** Law is in force, AG has enforced once (Instacart), retailers are lobbying and mapping pricing data per H&K/Faegre; but no vendor category exists yet and the 2d Cir. appeal is pending. Sale is to GCs via law firms; note that CT/NJ/MD bans (not disclosures) may make "audit" mean "prove we don't," which favors the anonymous-baseline product.
3. **Consumer subscription — weakest.** Massive salience (66–68% opposed) but every incumbent is free, Rocket Money's $7–14 is for budgeting, and the most visible harm (Instacart) has already been "fixed." Best use of the consumer app is as the data-collection layer for #1 and #2, likely free.
